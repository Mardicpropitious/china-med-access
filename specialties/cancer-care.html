Cancer Treatment in China

Surgical oncology · Radiotherapy · Chemotherapy · Multidisciplinary tumor boards

Cancer treatment in China is available to international patients through accredited oncology centers, primarily in Shanghai, Beijing and Guangzhou. Leading centers offer surgical oncology, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, immunotherapy and multidisciplinary tumor board (MDT) consultations — where surgeons, oncologists, radiologists and pathologists jointly design a treatment plan.

🎗️ Shanghai's Fudan University-affiliated cancer hospital and comprehensive hospitals (Zhongshan, Huashan, Ruijin) operate among China's largest oncology programs, with extensive experience in liver, lung, gastric, colorectal and pancreatic cancer surgery.

Which Cancers Are Commonly Treated

  • Liver cancer — surgical resection, ablation, TACE and systemic therapy.
  • Lung cancer — minimally invasive surgery, targeted therapy and radiotherapy.
  • Gastric & colorectal cancer — high-volume surgical programs with strong outcomes data.
  • Pancreatic cancer — complex resections at specialized centers.
  • Breast cancer — breast-conserving surgery, oncoplasty and systemic therapy.
  • Lymphoma and rare tumors — multidisciplinary management at academic centers.

Note: whether a specific case can be treated — and which approach is appropriate — is determined by a physician case review, not by this website.

How the Process Works for Cancer Patients

  1. Submit your records: pathology reports, imaging (CT/MRI/PET), surgical history and prior treatment records.
  2. Physician assessment: the hospital department evaluates whether your case is suitable, and whether additional pathology review (second opinion) is recommended.
  3. Treatment proposal: the hospital provides a written plan with cost estimate, timeline and risk explanation.
  4. Travel & treatment: after you decide, we coordinate visa documents, travel, admission and in-hospital interpretation.
  5. Follow-up: discharge package plus remote follow-up with your local oncologist.

Second Opinion & Remote Consultation

Many international patients begin with a remote second medical opinion — sending pathology and imaging for review by Chinese specialists before deciding to travel. This is coordinated by our team; the medical opinion itself is provided by qualified physicians at accredited institutions. It is not a diagnosis and does not replace care from your home-country physician.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can international patients receive cancer treatment in China?

Yes. Leading Chinese cancer centers with international patient departments accept overseas patients for surgical oncology, radiotherapy, chemotherapy and multidisciplinary tumor board consultations. Cases are first independently assessed by the treating physician.

What cancers are treated at Shanghai cancer centers?

Shanghai cancer centers commonly treat liver cancer, lung cancer, gastric cancer, colorectal cancer, pancreatic cancer, breast cancer, lymphoma and certain rare tumors, among others. Suitability is determined by a physician case review.

Can I get a second medical opinion without traveling?

Yes. Second-opinion coordination (pathology review and imaging assessment) can often be arranged remotely. The medical opinion is provided by qualified physicians at accredited institutions; we coordinate documents, translation and logistics.
